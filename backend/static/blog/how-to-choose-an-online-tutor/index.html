How to Choose an Online Tutor: 12 Questions to Ask Before You Pay

8 min read

In short

Choosing an online tutor is a high-stakes decision for a family. You are trusting a stranger with your child's learning, and the cost is significant - often thousands of dollars per year. The best way to reduce the risk is to ask the right questions before you commit. This guide gives you 12 essential questions, grouped by category, that reveal whether a tutor or platform is transparent, qualified and genuinely focused on your child's progress.

What does the buyer's anxiety feel like?

Every parent who has ever booked a tutor for the first time has felt the same thing: how do I know if this person is any good? The tutoring market is not well regulated. Tutors can set their own rates, describe their own qualifications and choose their own teaching methods without any external standard. A tutor who is excellent with Year 8 maths may be completely wrong for your Year 11 HSC Chemistry student, and there is no website that will tell you that until you have already paid for the first session.

The 12 questions below are grouped into four categories: qualifications and curriculum alignment, teaching approach, logistics and communication, and value and guarantees. Ask them in your initial conversation with any tutor or platform, and pay attention to how the answers are delivered. A qualified tutor who is used to working with parents will answer clearly and specifically. A tutor who is not used to parent questions will give vague answers, deflect or try to rush you into booking.

What are the 12 essential questions to ask?

Qualifications and curriculum alignment

These three questions establish whether the tutor actually knows the subject and the specific curriculum your child is following.

1. Are you familiar with the specific curriculum my child is following - the year level, the subject and the way assessments are structured? A tutor who knows trigonometry thoroughly but does not know the specific question formats and marking schemes for your child's exam system is leaving value on the table. For Australian parents, this means asking about HSC, VCE or NCEA specifics. For US parents, this means asking about Common Core, SAT or ACT alignment. For Canadian parents, this means asking about provincial curriculum differences.

2. What formal qualifications do you hold in this subject, and can you share evidence of your teaching experience? A university degree in the subject is the minimum bar for senior secondary tutoring. A teaching qualification, classroom experience or documented tutoring outcomes are stronger signals. Ask for specific evidence - a LinkedIn profile, a teaching registration number, references from previous families - rather than accepting a self-described title at face value.

3. How do you stay current with curriculum changes? Exam systems change. The HSC gets new calculus topics. The SAT goes digital. The NCEA updates its achievement standards. A tutor who is still teaching the 2019 version of the HSC Mathematics Advanced syllabus is doing your child a disservice. Ask how the tutor tracks and incorporates curriculum updates into their teaching.

Teaching approach

These three questions reveal whether the tutor's teaching style will suit your child's learning profile.

4. How do you structure a typical session, and what does the student do between sessions? A good tutor will describe a session structure that includes diagnostic check-ins, targeted instruction, guided practice and a clear takeaway for the student to work on independently. A tutor who describes sessions as going through the textbook or covering the content is not providing the structured, feedback-rich environment that produces measurable progress.

5. How do you adapt your teaching when a student does not understand a concept the first time? Every student has different learning preferences. A tutor who can explain the same concept in three different ways - visually, verbally and through worked examples - is more likely to reach your child than a tutor who has one fixed explanation style. Ask for a specific example of a time the tutor adapted their approach for a struggling student.

6. What does a typical progress report look like, and how do you communicate with parents between sessions? Progress is not just about grades. It is about confidence, engagement and the ability to work independently. A tutor who sends a brief written update after each session - what was covered, what the student found difficult, what to practise before the next session - is providing a level of transparency that most platforms do not offer. Ask whether these updates are included in the session price or charged separately.

Logistics and communication

These three questions establish the practical terms of the tutoring relationship.

7. What is your availability, and how do you handle session rescheduling and cancellations? A tutor who is fully booked may be excellent but unavailable when your child needs a pre-exam boost session. A tutor who charges for missed sessions without notice may be reliable but inflexible. Ask about the cancellation notice period, whether rescheduled sessions carry over to the next week and whether the tutor offers emergency session slots during exam periods.

8. What technology do you use, and what does the student need to have ready? Most online tutoring uses video conferencing with screen sharing and an interactive whiteboard. The tutor should be able to describe the tools clearly and provide setup instructions before the first session. Ask whether the tutor records sessions for review, whether they provide digital worksheets and whether the student needs a graphics tablet or just a mouse.

9. Do you require a minimum commitment, and what is your refund or rematch policy if the tutor is not a fit? The best answer is no minimum commitment and a free rematch if the tutor is not a fit within the first few sessions. The worst answer is a semester-long contract with no exceptions. Ask for the policy in writing before you pay anything.

Value and guarantees

These three questions establish whether the tutoring service is built for retention through quality or through lock-in.

10. How do you measure whether tutoring is working, and what happens if it is not? A provider that tracks progress with weekly reports and adjusts the learning plan based on results is signalling that they care about outcomes, not just session count. Ask for a concrete example of how the tutor or platform has adjusted a student's programme when progress was slower than expected.

11. What is included in the session price, and what costs extra? The answer should be a clear list: live session time, learning materials, progress reports, parent communication, practice tests if relevant. If the tutor says everything is included, ask for that in writing. If the tutor mentions add-on costs, ask for the prices before you commit.

12. Can the student keep the same tutor for the full programme, or is tutor continuity not guaranteed? This is the most important question for long-term programmes. A provider that guarantees tutor continuity is built for relationship-based learning. A provider that says we will try to keep the same tutor is built for marketplace matching. For a student who needs sustained support across a full year, the continuity guarantee is worth paying a premium for.

What red flags should make you walk away?

The tutor cannot name the specific exam board or curriculum your child is following. The tutor requires a full-semester or full-year payment before you have met them. The tutor cannot describe a structured session format and talks only about covering content. The tutor does not provide progress reports or parent communication, or charges extra for them. The platform or tutor uses high-pressure sales tactics: book now before the price goes up or I only have one slot left this week.

What green flags signal quality?

The tutor asks detailed questions about your child's current level, learning style and goals before quoting a price. The tutor can name the specific exam system, marking scheme and common student mistakes for your child's subject and year level. The tutor offers a free demo or trial session with no payment required, and the rematch policy is clear. The tutor describes a structured session format with diagnostic, instruction, practice and feedback components. The tutor provides written progress updates after each session as part of the standard service. The tutor guarantees continuity - the same tutor every week - and explains how that benefits the student.

The 12 questions above cover the four categories that matter most: qualifications and curriculum alignment, teaching approach, logistics and communication, and value and guarantees. The red flags and green flags give you a quick way to assess whether a tutor is transparent and focused on your child's progress.
